Pilot unit, built to order. Prototype design.

A Meshtastic-compatible LoRa radio and an Iridium satellite modem in one sealed case.

One satellite gateway for the whole team.

What it does

Compact v2 puts a Meshtastic-compatible LoRa radio and a RockBLOCK 9704 Iridium satellite modem in one sealed, 3D-printed case. It is designed as the satellite gateway for a team: every Meshtastic-compatible radio in LoRa range reaches the Iridium network through this one box, so the team buys one gateway and pays one airtime line instead of a satellite messenger and a plan for every person.

A phone with the MeshSat app for Android or iOS connects to the unit over Bluetooth and gets both radios. Without a phone, the unit routes its satellite channel on its own: a text typed on any radio in the mesh goes out over Iridium, and the reply comes back the same way.

Messages up to 100 kB travel over Iridium IMT. The quick-message button on the panel sends a preset message to a preset contact. There is no rescue service behind it.

What has been tested, and what has not

The design is a prototype. The node firmware with the RockBLOCK 9704 has been tested on the bench: the modem answers, registers and accepts a text, and the node routes a text from the mesh into the modem with no phone involved. No satellite message has been sent from a compact v2 yet. The earlier node, with a RockBLOCK 9603, has sent and received satellite messages from a garden. Range, battery life and the water rating of the case are not measured. The full record is the proven table in the node documentation; this page says no more than it does.

What it is not

MeshSat is not an emergency service and the unit is not a distress device. Do not rely on it to call for help. Message delivery is best effort: mesh radio and satellite links fail for reasons nobody controls, and nobody is watching on your behalf. Every satellite session is billed by the airtime provider, even one that only checks for new messages.

What is in the box

  • The unit: a LILYGO T-Beam Supreme over a Ground Control RockBLOCK 9704-SMA in the printed ASA case, with the display window, the quick-message button, two antenna sockets and a sealed USB-C socket
  • The helical Iridium antenna and the 868 MHz LoRa antenna
  • One 18650 Li-ion cell, 3.400 mAh
  • The instructions and safety leaflet and the EU declaration of conformity, in print

What you need besides the box

  • An Iridium IMT line for the modem, rented from Ground Control and billed by them. One line serves the whole team.
  • A Meshtastic-compatible LoRa radio for each person who sends from the mesh. Any will do; the unit itself is one.
  • The MeshSat app for Android or iOS, if you want to use the unit from a phone. The app is free and open source.

Open source

The firmware, the enclosure and the apps are open source: GPL-3.0 for the software, CERN-OHL-S-2.0 for the hardware. Everything is on github.com/meshsat. You can build one yourself; buying one assembled pays for the build, the testing and the documents.
